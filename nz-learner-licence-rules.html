After you pass

NZ car learner licence rules: supervisors and L plates

By NZ Learner · Updated 24 September 2026 · Class 1 cars

Passing the theory test gives you a foundation for supervised driving. Before each lesson, check both the conditions of your licence and whether the session suits your current experience.

In this guide

  1. You need a suitable supervisor
  2. Display L plates before driving
  3. Check the conditions for your actual licence
  4. Agree how the lesson will work
  5. Build up gradually
  6. Keep reliable guidance close

You need a suitable supervisor

NZTA says a learner’s supervisor must sit in the front passenger seat. They must hold a current, valid full New Zealand car licence without a supervisor condition, and have held it, or an equivalent overseas licence, for at least two years. Being an older friend or relative alone does not establish eligibility.

Official reference: NZTA — learner licence conditions

Display L plates before driving

Drive states that learner plates must be displayed at the front and back of the car. NZTA describes a black L on a yellow background and specifies the required dimensions. Check the official guidance when buying or making plates, and make sure they remain visible.

Official reference: NZTA — learner licence conditions

Check the conditions for your actual licence

Do not mix car-learner rules with motorcycle or restricted-licence rules found online. Read the current conditions and any restrictions recorded on your own licence. This article highlights two important requirements; it is not a complete list of every rule that applies to a driver or vehicle.

Agree how the lesson will work

Before moving off, discuss what you will practise, where you will go and how your supervisor will give instructions. Ask for directions with enough notice to understand them. Agree that you can stop somewhere safe if a situation becomes too demanding. A calm plan is more useful than trying to cover every skill at once.

Build up gradually

Start with tasks and conditions appropriate to your ability, with your supervisor’s guidance. After the session, write down one thing that improved and one thing to revisit. Connect what happened on the road with the relevant Road Code section. A question you understood on a screen may need more practice in a real driving situation.

Keep reliable guidance close

Use NZTA for licence conditions and Drive for supervised-learning resources. Check the current requirements before planning the next licence stage, rather than relying on old blog posts about waiting periods or age limits.

Official reference: Drive — after the learner test

Your next steps

  • Eligible supervisor beside you.
  • L plates displayed front and back.
  • Your licence conditions checked.
  • A route and lesson goal agreed.
  • Time afterwards to discuss what to practise next.
