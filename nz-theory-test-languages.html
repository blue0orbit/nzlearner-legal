Language & access

NZ learner theory test languages and reading support

By NZ Learner · Updated 24 September 2026 · Class 1 cars

Choose a study language that helps you understand the rules, then confirm how you will take the official test. These are two separate decisions.

In this guide

  1. Check the official language list
  2. Ask about reading or interpreting support
  3. Use translations to build understanding
  4. App languages are a separate feature
  5. Prepare a short list of questions for your agent

Check the official language list

NZTA lists English plus Arabic, Chinese (simplified and traditional), Fijian, Filipino, Gujarati, Hindi, Korean, Māori, Punjabi, Samoan, Thai and Tongan for the theory test. Check the live NZTA page and tell your agent which language you need.

Official reference: NZTA — theory test and language support

Ask about reading or interpreting support

NZTA describes reader support in English and possible interpreter arrangements. Requirements apply to interpreters; do not assume any accompanying person can interpret your test. Discuss your needs with the agent early so you understand what must be arranged.

Official reference: NZTA — theory test and language support

Use translations to build understanding

When studying in a familiar language, pay attention to the meaning of terms rather than learning an answer by shape or length. Keep a small vocabulary list for road signs and instructions you find confusing. Compare explanations with the Road Code and ask for clarification when a translated sentence seems unclear.

App languages are a separate feature

The current NZ Learner build contains English and ten additional languages: Māori, Samoan, Simplified Chinese, Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, Polish, Portuguese, Romanian and Ukrainian. These cover the interface, questions, answers and explanations. Coverage is complete; independent language and content checks remain in progress. This list does not describe languages offered by the official test.

Prepare a short list of questions for your agent

Ask whether the language you want is available, how to request it, and what arrangements are needed for reading or interpreting support. Confirm whether those arrangements affect when you should attend. Avoid leaving this conversation until you are about to start the test.

Your next steps

  • Choose your preferred study language.
  • Check the current official test-language list.
  • Contact the agent about support before test day.
  • Report unclear app translations through support.

Independent guidance, not a licensing service. Check current official requirements with NZTA and your driver licensing agent. App results do not guarantee an official test result.
